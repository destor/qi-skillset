# Naive questions by area

For each area: naive questions that go deep. The misconceptions to listen for are in `misconceptions.md`.

The questions below are seeds, written short. When asking one, give it the shape set out in the skill body: a frame of one to three sentences, then an open question that takes a few sentences to answer.

## States and measurement (N-C Ch 1, 2; Preskill Ch 2, 3)

- "If it is both 0 and 1, why do I only ever see one of them?"
- "How would I tell, in the lab, a superposition from simply not knowing?"
- "What makes something a measurement? What is special about the detector?"

## Noise and open systems (N-C Ch 8; Preskill Ch 3)

- "Where did the information go?"
- "Could you get it back if you were clever?"
- "Why does the qubit fall down and not jump up?"
- "Why is the decay exponential?"

## Circuits and algorithms (N-C Ch 3 to 6; Preskill Ch 5, 6)

- "If you cannot read out the superposition, what was the point of making it?"
- "Where exactly do the wrong answers go?"
- "Why can't an ordinary computer do the same by guessing at random?"
- "Doesn't the oracle already know the answer?"

## Entanglement and protocols (N-C §1.3, §2.3, §2.6, §12.6; Preskill Ch 4)

- "How is that different from two gloves in two boxes?"
- "If nothing travels, how does the other photon know?"
- "Why does Alice still have to phone Bob?"

## Error correction (N-C Ch 10; Preskill Ch 7)

- "You said copying is impossible. How is this not copying?"
- "How can you find the error without looking at the qubit?"
- "Errors can be any size. How can a few corrections cover all of them?"
- "Who corrects the correcting circuit?"

## Distance, entropy and information (N-C Ch 9, 11, 12; Preskill Ch 10)

- "So how many bits is a qubit worth?"
- "How can a part be more uncertain than the whole?"
- "What do you actually do in the lab to get a fidelity?"
